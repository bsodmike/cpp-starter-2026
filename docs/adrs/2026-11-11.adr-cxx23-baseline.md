# C++23 as the language baseline, with C++26 opt-in

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-11-11

## Context

WG21 finished C++26 technical work in March 2026, but the ISO DIS ballot failed
in late October 2026 over Contracts, and WG21 meets on 16 November 2026 to decide
what happens next. The final shape of C++26 is therefore still open.

Separately, the compilers available to us span a wide range. GitHub-hosted
runners carry GCC up to 15.2 and Clang up to 22.1; the newest releases (GCC 16.2,
Clang 23.1) are not on a hosted image.

## Decision

Target C++23 with `CMAKE_CXX_STANDARD 23`, `CMAKE_CXX_STANDARD_REQUIRED ON` and
`CMAKE_CXX_EXTENSIONS OFF`. Express the requirement as
`target_compile_features(acme_options INTERFACE cxx_std_23)` so it propagates to
consumers of the installed package.

C++26 is available per build through `ACME_ENABLE_CXX26`, which adds
`cxx_std_26` and defines `ACME_HAS_CXX26` on `acme::build_options` only. Because
that target is build-tree-only, a C++26 feature can never leak into a public
header and force the standard on a consumer.

Enforce these compiler floors in `cmake/CompilerFloor.cmake`, failing configure
below them unless `ACME_ALLOW_UNSUPPORTED_COMPILER=ON`:

| Compiler | Floor |
| --- | --- |
| GCC | 14 |
| Clang | 21 |
| AppleClang | 17.0 |
| MSVC | 19.50 (toolset 14.50) |

## Consequences

- The project gets `std::expected`, `std::format` and `std::print` without
  waiting on the C++26 ballot. `acme::core` uses `std::expected` for parse
  failures today.
- GCC 13 and earlier are excluded, which is deliberate: GCC 13 was the first
  release with `std::format`, and 14 is the first we are willing to support.
- The AppleClang floor is expressed as an Apple Clang version, not an Xcode
  version, because the two are unrelated: Xcode 26.x reports AppleClang 21.0.0.
  An Xcode-derived floor such as 26.4 is satisfiable by no shipping Xcode and
  rejects every Mac. Observed on Xcode 26 during the initial build.
- The floor check needs an escape hatch, because a contributor on a distro
  compiler should get a clear message and a documented override rather than a
  wall of template errors. The override warns loudly.
- Revisit after WG21's decision on the failed ballot. Nothing here blocks
  moving to C++26 later; the opt-in path exists to trial features in the
  meantime.
